\subsection{Knowledge Sharing}
\label{subsec:knowledge-sharing-results}

We run the full analysis of Section~\ref{subsec:knowledge-sharing} on all eight
bilingual 30B-token finals (\texttt{en-\{de,fr,ar,zh\}} $\times$
\{fair, starved\}) over the 2{,}039 PolyFact facts. All checkpoints are cooled
finals at the same token budget, so every comparison below is free of the
learning-rate-state confound. Per model, between 646 and 888 facts are answered
correctly in both training languages (capped at 800 for attribution);
own-language accuracy is $.39$--$.54$ against $.25$ chance.

\paragraph{Conductivity.}
Table~\ref{tab:conductivity} reports the overlap of correctly answered facts
(Eq.~\ref{eq:jaccard}) together with the floor expected if correctness were
\emph{independent} across the two languages,
$J^{\mathrm{indep}}_{s,t} = a_s a_t / (a_s + a_t - a_s a_t)$, which rises with
accuracy and must therefore be subtracted before comparing conditions. Three
observations. First, conductivity is far above independence in every pair
(observed $J = .54$--$.73$ against floors of $.28$--$.35$): knowing a fact in
one language strongly predicts knowing it in the other, for every partner and
both tokenizers. Second, the excess over the floor is only mildly
script-dependent (same-script $+.34$--$.40$, cross-script $+.26$--$.31$) --- a
contrast worth keeping in mind for the parametric results below, where the
script gap is a factor of $3$--$8$. Third, the fair tokenizer yields higher
conductivity than the starved one for \emph{all four} partners, and the
ordering survives subtracting each model's own independence floor (e.g.\ fr
$+.404$ vs.\ $+.344$), so it is not an artefact of the fair models simply
answering more facts correctly.

\paragraph{Parametric sharing.}
Figure~\ref{fig:kn-sharing}a reports $\Delta$KS: the same-fact cross-language
Jaccard overlap of the top-100 knowledge neurons (Eq.~\ref{eq:neuron-overlap})
\emph{minus} the mismatched-fact baseline. The baseline is substantial
(unrelated facts share $J \approx .09$--$.15$ through generically important
neurons) and differs between tokenizers, so raw overlap is not comparable
across conditions; $\Delta$KS is. Two results. \emph{Script dominates
sharing:} same-script pairs share $2$--$5\times$ more fact-specific neurons
than cross-script pairs under both tokenizers (fair: de $.203$, fr $.135$
vs.\ ar $.072$, zh $.027$), even though their conductivity is nearly flat ---
the same consistency-without-sharing dissociation \citet{ifergan2024beneath}
report for off-the-shelf 7B models, reproduced here in controlled bilinguals
where corpus, budget and architecture are held fixed. Because $\approx\!42\%$
of de/fr facts have byte-identical gold surface forms in both languages (Latin
entity names), which inflates same-script overlap $\sim\!2.6\times$, we verify
the script gap on the different-surface-form subset only; it persists (fair:
de $.122$, fr $.092$ vs.\ ar $.072$, zh $.025$). \emph{The fair tokenizer
increases parametric sharing:} $\Delta$KS is higher under fair for de
($+.060$ $[.053, .067]$), fr ($+.025$ $[.019, .030]$) and ar ($+.047$
$[.043, .051]$; paired bootstrap over the fact intersection, 95\% CIs), and
unchanged for zh ($+.001$ $[-.002, .005]$). All results are stable for
$K \in \{50, 100, 200, 500\}$.

\paragraph{Causal validation.}
Before reading the transfer results, Figure~\ref{fig:kn-ablation} establishes
that the attributed neurons are causally load-bearing: zeroing a fact's own
top-100 neurons ($0.11\%$ of the 90{,}112 FFN neurons) reduces the gold
answer's log-likelihood by $8.5$--$18.5$ nats and flips the predicted answer
for $51$--$87\%$ of facts, while zeroing 100 \emph{random} neurons costs
$0.002$--$0.033$ nats and flips essentially none. The different-fact controls
sit in between, confirming that part of any top-100's effect flows through
generically important neurons --- exactly the component the baselines in both
measures subtract.

\paragraph{Causal transfer.}
Figure~\ref{fig:kn-sharing}b shows the ablation transfer rate: for a fact
known in both languages, the damage to language~$t$ caused by zeroing
language~$s$'s top-100 (net of the different-fact control), divided by the
damage caused by $t$'s own top-100 (net of its control). The ratio is
dimensionless, which makes it comparable across tokenizers whose summed
log-likelihoods span different numbers of tokens and whose independently
trained loss surfaces have different damage scales. The ordering mirrors the
overlap measure: same-script transfer is high ($.55$--$.78$), cross-script
transfer is low ($.10$--$.33$), and the fair tokenizer increases it for fr
($+.156$ $[.112, .200]$) and ar ($+.236$ $[.190, .282]$), with de positive
but not significant ($+.033$ $[-.005, .074]$) and zh unchanged ($+.023$
$[-.045, .094]$). Under the starved tokenizer the \texttt{en-ar} model stores
a fact it can retrieve in both languages in almost disjoint parameters
(transfer rate $.098$); de-starving the tokenizer more than triples the
functional sharing.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Three points. (i)~\emph{Vocabulary allocation shapes where knowledge lives}:
fair $\geq$ starved in all eight (partner $\times$ measure) cells,
significantly in five, on two methodologically independent measures (gradient
attribution overlap and causal ablation). (ii)~\emph{The effect is
Arabic-led, not script-general}: Arabic has both the largest starved-fertility
penalty ($1.476\times$, the worst of our languages) and by far the largest
sharing gain from fair allocation, while Chinese --- equally cross-script,
but with a mild fertility penalty --- shows none. Starvation does not tax
cross-script pairs as a class at the parameter level; it taxes the pair where
the tokenizer damage is concentrated. (iii)~\emph{Sharing and capability
dissociate}: these checkpoints show no corresponding gap in attained
capability or transfer deltas on our benchmark suite, yet differ by up to
$3.4\times$ in parametric sharing --- storing the same fact twice evidently
costs little at this scale, mirroring the consistency-vs-sharing dissociation
at the behavioral level. Finally, the pair with the least sharing is also
organized differently in depth: $66\%$ of Chinese knowledge neurons sit in
layers 0--3 under the fair tokenizer (Figure~\ref{fig:kn-layers}), against
$\approx\!28\%$ for de/fr, consistent with our representation-level finding
that cross-script processing is handled by embedding-adjacent machinery.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
One training run per condition, as throughout this work; the Arabic effect is
the largest we measure and is doubly attested (overlap and ablation agree),
but a seed replicate would harden it. Fair and starved twins are measured on
partly different fact sets (intersection $J = .62$--$.71$); paired analysis on
the intersection agrees with the full-set analysis everywhere. Attribution
uses a joint-path integrated-gradients estimator over all layers
(Section~\ref{subsec:knowledge-sharing}); its top-100 ranking is stable to a
$5\times$ finer integration grid (Jaccard $.80$), and the causal results do
not depend on the attribution flavour. Neuron granularity cannot rule out
polysemantic neurons implementing two language-specific features, and
zero-ablation is an off-distribution intervention with known backup-circuit
caveats.
